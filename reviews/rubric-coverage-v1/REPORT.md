# Coverage against the supplied rubric

The current pilot tests source fidelity, specified decisions and house-style fit on eight short synthetic briefs. It does not yet establish the best model for business writing or strategy consulting generally. The table below is an author coverage assessment, not a new score or a grading change.

| Original dimension | Current coverage | Material limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Promise / commitment fidelity | Explicit scope, start, approval and delivery conditions appear in the briefs; grounding checks also catch invented commitments. | No long SOW with interacting payment, liability, staffing and service-level clauses. |
| Evidence grounding | Every brief includes the grounding criterion; target-versus-result and causality checks add task-specific coverage. | No supplied citation excerpts or real-link support traps. URL hygiene and citation entailment are untested. |
| Numbers integrity | Vendor totals, fee/rate arithmetic, thresholds, observed reductions and target-versus-actual distinctions are specified. | No independent output-arithmetic recomputation. Schedule arithmetic and complex prose/table reconciliation are not systematically tested. The readout calculation-omission gate has a documented prompt mismatch. |
| Internal consistency | Launch timing and pre/post-acceptance ownership have explicit checks; grounding review can catch other contradictions. | No deliberately conflicting source packets or long-document cross-section stress test. |
| Decision readiness | Briefs require recommendations, decisions, prerequisites and follow-up actions under stated constraints. | Most decisions are constrained by a clear mandatory gate. Complex strategic tradeoffs and recommendation quality beyond those gates are not separately measured. |
| Language precision | Commitment, owner and dependency checks plus the style lens assess some material ambiguity. Ambiguous signatory and reassurance cases remain unresolved. | No separately scored precision dimension or independent calibration of will/should/may and implied obligations. |
| Anti-slop / Claudespeak | The frozen 29-category lens is reviewed, and character/candidate scans are saved. Factual-contrast exceptions are applied. | Keyword matches alone are not defects. Short synthetic briefs do not prove coverage of every rhetorical pattern or writing genre; judgment remains unblinded. |
| MECE accountability (when applicable) | The handoff brief separates defect fixes from incident ownership and exposes an unassigned rehearsal organizer. | No broad responsibility matrix with overlapping roles and multiple transitions. Applicability/N/A behavior is not independently tested. |

## Method differences and unvalidated parts

- The original draft proposed severity weighting. Current reports deliberately show separate check counts, content readiness, style findings and unresolved judgments; they do not implement a validated severity-weighted score. The original weighting text is ambiguous and specifies no actual weights.
- Jev has been disabled at the user's request. Current semantic grading is direct assistant review, with exact evidence and preserved corrections. It is not independent judge validation.
- The original human-gold layer remains unfulfilled. The ten-case calibration packet contains prior assistant labels and no independent reviewer responses.
- Current arithmetic and contradiction findings are semantic reviews against explicit checks. Deterministic scans cover characters, candidates, word count and some residue; they do not automatically establish arithmetic or entailment correctness.
- Base-model calls have no browsing or tool access, so the rubric example about unauthorized web verification of confidential facts is outside this harness's behavior space.
- Four-slide Markdown outlines are the tested artifact, not rendered presentation design. These are short single-call deliverables; the original iterative quality-erosion inspiration is not tested by this one-shot protocol.
