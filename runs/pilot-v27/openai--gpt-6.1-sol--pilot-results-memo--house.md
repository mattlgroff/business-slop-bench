## Pilot scaling decision

Recommendation: Do not scale now. Authorize a matched-ticket follow-up to test whether the handling-time improvement holds and quality meets the required threshold.

The pilot’s 100 tickets had a mean handling time of 12 minutes, compared with 15 minutes across 200 baseline tickets. That is an observed reduction of 3 minutes, or 20%. Ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized, so the results do not establish that the pilot caused the improvement.

Quality pass rate fell from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot, 3 percentage points below the 92% scaling threshold. The pilot therefore fails the current quality requirement. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured.

The results support further testing of the observed handling-time improvement. They do not support scaling or claiming cash savings.

The next decision is for the COO to authorize Sam to run the matched-ticket follow-up. Use that follow-up to reassess handling time and quality before reconsidering scale; a quality pass rate of at least 92% remains a required condition.
