## Recommendation: Do Not Scale Now

### What the Results Support

| Metric | Baseline | Pilot | Verdict |
|--------|----------|-------|---------|
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min | Promising but unproven |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% | **Fails 92% threshold** |
| Measured cost savings | — | None | Not yet demonstrated |

The pilot shows a 3-minute handling-time improvement, but this cannot be treated as a causal effect. Allocation was not randomized and the ticket mix differed from baseline, so the observed gap may reflect easier tickets rather than process gain.

More critically, quality dropped to 89%, breaching the 92% scaling threshold. This is a hard gate regardless of the time result.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so no financial case for scaling exists today.

### What the Results Do Not Support

- Scaling the pilot as-is.
- Claiming the time improvement is attributable to the intervention.
- Asserting a positive ROI.

### Next Decision

**COO decision required:** authorize a matched-ticket follow-up (Sam's proposed design) to isolate whether the handling-time gain is real once ticket mix is controlled.

If quality cannot be held at or above 92% in a controlled follow-up, the scaling threshold fails on both speed and quality grounds, and the pilot should be closed without scale.

### Summary

One metric improved but is confounded by design. A gating metric failed outright. No savings are measured. The correct move is a controlled follow-up, not a scale decision.
