# Executive Readout: Ticket Automation Pilot

**Recommendation: Do not scale now.** Authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up instead.

## What the results show

The pilot cut mean handling time from 15 minutes (baseline, 200 tickets) to 12 minutes (pilot, 100 tickets), a 3-minute improvement. Two factors prevent crediting that gain to the pilot itself:

- The ticket mix differed between baseline and pilot.
- Allocation was not randomized.

A different or easier mix could account for part or all of the time savings.

## Quality failed the scaling threshold

Quality pass rate fell from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot. The required threshold for scaling is at least 92%. The pilot missed by 3 points, a clear bar it did not clear regardless of the handling-time result.

## No cost benefit measured

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured. Scaling now would commit spend based on unproven savings and a quality regression.

## Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up. That follow-up controls for ticket mix, which addresses the main flaw in the pilot's time comparison. If it confirms a handling-time gain while quality meets the 92% threshold, the scaling case can be revisited with valid evidence.

**Requested action: COO authorizes the matched-ticket follow-up and defers scaling.**
