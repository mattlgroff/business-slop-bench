# Pilot Results: Recommendation on Scaling

## Recommendation
Do not scale now. Authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up and have the COO decide on scaling after that data is in.

## What the results support
The pilot handled 100 tickets at 12 minutes average, versus 15 minutes for 200 tickets at baseline. The comparison is not reliable: the pilot's ticket mix differed from baseline and tickets were not randomly allocated, so the 3-minute gap may reflect easier tickets rather than a faster process.

Quality fell from 94% to 89%, below the 92% threshold required to scale. This result stands on its own and does not depend on the allocation issue.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no cost case yet, independent of the speed and quality questions.

## Next decision
Sam can run a matched-ticket follow-up that controls for ticket mix and uses random allocation. This would tell us whether the 12-minute result holds on comparable tickets and whether quality can be brought back to 92% or above.

The COO needs to decide whether to authorize that follow-up. Until quality clears the threshold on a matched sample, scaling is not supported by the current data.
