# Pilot scaling readout

**Recommendation: hold scaling.** The pilot does not meet the required quality threshold, so the evidence does not support a scale-up decision now.

## What the results show

Handling time fell from a 15-minute mean at baseline (200 tickets) to 12 minutes in the pilot (100 tickets). The pilot covered a different ticket mix and allocation was randomized. Ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized, so we cannot attribute the 3-minute difference to the change under test.

Quality sets the gate. Pass rate dropped from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot. The scaling threshold is at least 92%, and the pilot misses it by 3 points. Quality below the gate blocks scaling regardless of handling-time gains.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured. A scale-up decision would rest on handling-time change we cannot attribute and quality that fails the gate, with no validated cost case.

## Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up. That study pairs comparable tickets across conditions and answers the open question: does the change produce the handling-time gain once ticket mix is held steady, and does quality hold at or above 92%?

If authorized, the follow-up returns the evidence needed for a scaling decision. If declined, the record stands as is, and scaling is unsupported because quality is below threshold and the handling-time result is confounded by unrandomized allocation across differing ticket mixes.
