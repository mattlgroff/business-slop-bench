# Executive Readout: Pilot Scaling Decision

## Recommendation
**Do not scale the pilot now.** Authorize Sam's proposed matched-ticket follow-up before making a scaling decision.

## What the Results Support
- **Speed improvement looks promising but unconfirmed**: The pilot showed a 3-minute reduction in mean handling time (12 min vs. 15 min baseline). However, this comparison is confounded — ticket mix differed between groups and allocation was not randomized. We cannot attribute the speed gain to the new process with confidence.
- **Quality fell below the scaling threshold**: Pilot quality pass rate was 89%, falling short of the required 92% threshold (baseline was 94%). This alone is a disqualifying result under current scaling criteria.
- **No financial case established**: No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no cost justification to accompany any efficiency claims.

## Why Scaling Now Is Not Justified
Two of the three pillars needed to support scaling — validated performance improvement and quality assurance — are either unproven (due to methodology issues) or failing (quality below threshold). The third pillar, cost savings, has not been measured at all. Scaling under these conditions would mean committing resources without evidence that the pilot's apparent speed gain is real, and while knowingly operating below the quality bar.

## Next Decision
The next step is not a scale/no-scale choice — it is a **methodology decision**: whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up study. This follow-up would control for ticket mix and randomization issues, producing a cleaner read on whether the speed improvement is genuine and whether quality can be brought to threshold.

**Action requested of COO**: Approve or deny the matched-ticket follow-up. Scaling should not be revisited until that data is in hand.
