**Executive Readout: Pilot Scaling Decision**

**Recommendation: Do not scale now. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up.**

**What the results support**

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot | Threshold | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min | — | 3 min faster |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% | ≥92% | Below threshold |
| Cash savings | — | None measured | — | Unknown |

The pilot shows a 3-minute reduction in mean handling time. The quality pass rate fell from 94% to 89%, below the required 92% scaling threshold. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured.

**Why the pilot cannot support scaling**

Ticket mix differed between baseline and pilot, and allocation was not randomized. The 3-minute handling-time improvement cannot be attributed to the pilot intervention. The quality decline may reflect the mix difference, the intervention, or both. The data cannot separate these.

**Next decision**

Sam can run a matched-ticket follow-up. The COO decides whether to authorize it.

If authorized, the follow-up should match tickets on the mix variables that differed, so handling time and quality can be compared on equivalent work. The COO should set the follow-up's pass criteria before it starts: quality at or above 92%, and a handling-time gain that holds on matched tickets.

Scale only if the follow-up clears both.
