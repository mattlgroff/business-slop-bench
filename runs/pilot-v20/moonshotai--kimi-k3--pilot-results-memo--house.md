# Executive readout: do not scale the pilot now

## Recommendation

Do not authorize scaling. The pilot fails the required quality threshold and has produced no measured cost savings. The next decision belongs to the COO: authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up, or close the initiative.

## What the results support

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot | Scaling bar |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mean handling time | 15 min (200 tickets) | 12 min (100 tickets) | None set |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% | At least 92% |

The results support three claims:

1. Handling time fell by 3 minutes per ticket in the pilot. This figure does not establish a real efficiency gain, because the ticket mix differed between the two periods and allocation was not randomized. The 12-minute result may reflect easier tickets rather than a better process.
2. Quality degraded. The pass rate dropped from 94% to 89%, three points below the 92% threshold required for scaling. On the stated criteria alone, the pilot does not qualify to expand.
3. No financial benefit has been demonstrated. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so the 3-minute time difference has produced no confirmed value.

The results do not support any claim that the pilot caused the faster handling time, that quality will recover at scale, or that the approach saves money.

## Risk of scaling now

Scaling would lock in a process that misses its quality bar, on evidence that cannot separate the tool's effect from ticket mix. If the 89% pass rate persists at higher volume, the defect count grows with it.

## Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up. That study is the available path to a valid comparison: matching tickets controls for mix, which is the main confounder in the current data. A follow-up should also re-measure the quality pass rate against the 92% threshold and track whether any staffing or cash saving actually occurs.

Recommendation to the COO: authorize the matched-ticket follow-up. It is the only proposed step that can produce evidence capable of supporting a scale decision. If the follow-up shows the time gain holds at comparable quality, scaling can be reconsidered then. If it does not, close the pilot.
