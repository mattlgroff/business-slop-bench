# Pilot Scaling Readout

**Recommendation: Do not scale now. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up.**

## What the results support

Mean handling time fell from 15 minutes (baseline, 200 tickets) to 12 minutes (pilot, 100 tickets). Because the pilot's ticket mix differed and ticket allocation was not randomized, the 3-minute gap cannot be attributed to the pilot change with confidence.

Quality fell below the scaling threshold. The pass rate was 94% at baseline and 89% in the pilot, against a required minimum of 92% for scaling. This alone blocks a scale decision.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so the pilot has no verified financial benefit to offset the quality shortfall.

## Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize a matched-ticket follow-up, which Sam can run. A matched-ticket design would hold ticket mix constant and address the non-randomized allocation, testing whether the handling-time gain holds at or above the 92% quality threshold.

The scale decision should wait for those results.
