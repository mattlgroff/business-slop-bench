# Executive Readout: AI Ticket-Handling Pilot

**Recommendation: Do not scale now. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up.**

## What the results support

**Speed improved, but the comparison is not trustworthy.** Mean handling time fell from 15 minutes (baseline, 200 tickets) to 12 minutes (pilot, 100 tickets). However, the ticket mix differed between the two groups and allocation was not randomized. The observed 3-minute improvement may reflect easier tickets in the pilot, not a genuine tool effect. We cannot attribute the gain to the intervention.

**Quality fell below the scaling threshold.** The quality pass rate was 94% at baseline and 89% in the pilot. The required threshold for scaling is at least 92%. The pilot result fails this gate on its face — and given the uncontrolled ticket mix, the true quality effect could be even worse on a comparable mix.

**No financial case has been demonstrated.** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no cost-benefit evidence to justify a broader rollout.

## Next decision

The **COO must decide whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up study**. This is the critical path: it would control for ticket mix and produce a defensible comparison of both handling time and quality pass rate.

**Recommended conditions for scaling later:**
1. Follow-up shows handling time improvement holds on matched tickets.
2. Quality pass rate meets or exceeds 92%.
3. A measured cost or capacity benefit is established.

Until those conditions are met, the pilot's evidence is insufficient to justify scaling.
