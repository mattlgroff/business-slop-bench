# Executive Readout: Scale the Pilot?

**Recommendation: Do not scale now. Run Sam's matched-ticket follow-up first.**

## What the results support

Handling time fell from 15 minutes (baseline, 200 tickets) to 12 minutes (pilot, 100 tickets). The 20% reduction looks favorable on speed, but three conditions block a scaling decision.

**Speed benefit is not isolated.** Ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized between groups. A shorter average can come from easier tickets or from self-selection, not from the pilot treatment alone. Sam's matched-ticket design addresses this bias directly.

**Quality is below the bar.** Pilot pass rate was 89%; the scaling threshold requires at least 92%. Baseline was 94%. The pilot sits 5 points below threshold and 5 points below baseline. A faster time does not offset a quality miss on a defined rule.

**No cost case.** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured. Scaling would authorize spend without a measured return.

## Next decision

COO authorizes Sam to run the matched-ticket follow-up. That study re-tests handling time and quality endpoints with matched ticket types and randomized allocation. If quality clears 92% on matched tickets at acceptable cost, scale. If quality fails again, redesign the pilot before retesting.
