**Executive Readout: Pilot Scaling Recommendation**

**Recommendation:** Do not scale the pilot now. Authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up to generate decision-grade evidence.

**What the results show**

| Metric | Baseline | Pilot | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tickets | 200 | 100 | — |
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min | — |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% | ≥92% required |
| Measured cash savings | None | None | — |

**What the results support**

- Handling time fell by 3 minutes (20%) in the pilot sample. This is a promising signal, not a proven effect.
- The pilot ticket mix differed from baseline and allocation was not randomized. The time reduction may reflect easier tickets rather than the intervention.
- Quality pass rate dropped 5 points to 89%, below the 92% scaling threshold. On this criterion alone, the pilot does not qualify to scale.
- No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no demonstrated financial return.

**What the results do not support**

- A causal claim that the pilot process reduces handling time.
- Any claim of cost savings.
- Meeting the quality bar required for scale.

**Risks of scaling now**

- Locking in a quality decline below threshold across the full ticket volume.
- Committing resources against an unmeasured financial benefit.
- Attributing a mix-driven time difference to the process change.

**Next decision (COO)**

Authorize Sam to run a matched-ticket follow-up. This should compare like-for-like tickets to isolate the process effect and confirm whether quality can be held at or above 92% while preserving the time gain. Cost impact should be measured explicitly.

**Decision rule for the follow-up:** Scale only if the matched comparison confirms a handling-time reduction and quality pass rate is ≥92%. If quality remains below threshold, stop or redesign.
