Executive readout: pilot scaling decision

Recommendation: do not scale now. Authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up.

What the results support

The pilot cut mean handling time from 15 minutes (200 tickets) to 12 minutes (100 tickets). That 20% reduction cannot be attributed to the pilot itself. The ticket mix differed from baseline and allocation was not randomized, so easier tickets may explain some or all of the gain.

Quality moved the wrong way. The pass rate fell from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot, three points below the 92% threshold required for scaling. On the current evidence the pilot fails the scaling criterion regardless of how the time savings are interpreted.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so the business case rests on an unadjusted time metric and nothing else.

What would change the recommendation

Scaling becomes defensible if a follow-up with comparable ticket mix shows the handling time gain holds and the pass rate reaches 92% or higher. If the quality gap persists on matched tickets, the pilot should be redesigned or stopped rather than expanded.

The next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam to run the matched-ticket follow-up. The follow-up should hold ticket mix constant between arms, record pass rate against the 92% threshold, and, where possible, capture any staffing or cash effect so the next readout can address cost directly.

Approving the follow-up commits no scaling resources and gives the COO the evidence needed to make the scale decision on attributable results.
