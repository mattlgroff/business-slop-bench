**Pilot Scaling Recommendation**

**Decision: Do not scale the pilot now.**

The handling-time reduction (15 min to 12 min) is promising but insufficient to justify scaling given three unresolved problems.

**What the results show**

| Metric | Baseline (n=200) | Pilot (n=100) |
|---|---|---|
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% |

1. Quality fell below the 92% threshold required for scaling. The pilot's 89% pass rate represents a 5-percentage-point drop from baseline.
2. Ticket mix differed between baseline and pilot, and allocation was not randomized. The handling-time improvement may reflect easier tickets, not process improvement.
3. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no confirmed cost benefit.

**Next decision**

Sam can run a matched-ticket follow-up that controls for ticket mix. The COO should authorize this study before any scaling commitment. If the follow-up shows handling time at or below 12 minutes with a quality pass rate at or above 92% on comparable tickets, scaling has a defensible basis.
