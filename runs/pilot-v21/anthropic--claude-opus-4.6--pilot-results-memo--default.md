# Executive Readout: Pilot Scale Decision

**Recommendation: Do not scale. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up study.**

---

## Results Summary

| Metric | Baseline | Pilot | Threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tickets | 200 | 100 | — |
| Mean Handling Time | 15 min | 12 min | — |
| Quality Pass Rate | 94% | 89% | ≥ 92% |
| Cash Savings | — | None measured | — |

## What the Results Support

The pilot shows a 3-minute reduction in mean handling time. However, **three issues prevent a scale recommendation**:

1. **Quality is below threshold.** The pilot's 89% pass rate falls short of the 92% minimum. This is disqualifying on its own.
2. **No proven cost savings.** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so the business case for scaling is unsubstantiated.
3. **Unreliable comparison.** Ticket mix differed between groups and allocation was not randomized. The handling-time improvement may reflect easier tickets, not a real process gain.

## Next Decision

Sam can run a matched-ticket follow-up that would control for ticket mix and strengthen causal claims. **The COO should authorize this study.** If the follow-up shows handling-time improvement holds on comparable tickets *and* quality meets the 92% threshold, the case for scaling can be revisited.
