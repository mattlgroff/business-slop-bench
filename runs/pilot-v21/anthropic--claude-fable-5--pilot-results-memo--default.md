# Executive Readout: Pilot Scaling Decision

## Recommendation
**Do not scale now.** Authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up instead.

## What the Results Show

| Metric | Baseline | Pilot | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tickets | 200 | 100 | Adequate for a pilot |
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min | Directionally favorable |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% | **Below 92% scaling threshold** |
| Measured cash savings | — | None | Unproven |

## What the Results Support
- A **promising signal**: a 3-minute (20%) reduction in mean handling time.
- Justification for **further, more rigorous testing**.

## What the Results Do Not Support
- **Attribution of the time savings.** Ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized, so the improvement may reflect easier tickets rather than the intervention.
- **Meeting the quality bar.** Pilot quality (89%) fell below the required 92% threshold — a hard blocker on scaling regardless of speed gains.
- **A financial case.** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured.

## Risks of Scaling Now
- Locking in a quality decline below the required threshold.
- Investing in a change whose benefit may disappear under a comparable ticket mix.

## Next Decision
The decision before the COO is **not** whether to scale — the quality shortfall and confounded design rule that out today. The decision is **whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up**, which would:
1. Control for ticket mix, isolating the true handling-time effect.
2. Test whether quality can meet or exceed 92% under the new approach.

**Recommended action:** Approve the matched-ticket follow-up. Revisit the scaling decision when it delivers (a) a mix-controlled handling-time result and (b) quality at or above threshold, alongside a plan to measure actual cash savings.
