# Support Pilot Readout: Handling Time and Quality

**Recommendation: do not scale now. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up.**

## What the pilot showed

Mean handling time was 12 minutes across 100 pilot tickets, against 15 minutes across 200 baseline tickets. The ticket mix differed between the two groups and allocation was not randomized, so the 3-minute gap cannot be attributed to the pilot method. Easier tickets may have landed in the pilot group.

Quality pass rate fell to 89% in the pilot from 94% at baseline. The scaling threshold is 92%. The pilot is 3 points below the bar on the one criterion already agreed for this decision.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no financial case to weigh against the quality shortfall.

## What the results support

The results support one conclusion: the pilot did not meet the quality threshold, and the speed difference remains unverified. They do not support a scaling decision in either direction, because the comparison groups are not equivalent.

## Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam to run a matched-ticket follow-up. Matching tickets across the two methods would isolate the handling-time difference and give a second read on quality pass rate under comparable conditions.

If authorized, the follow-up should report mean handling time and quality pass rate on matched tickets, plus any measured staffing effect. Scaling returns to the table when the pass rate reaches 92% or higher on matched tickets.

If the COO declines the follow-up, the pilot closes at the current result and the current process stays in place.
