# Pilot readout: scale decision

**Recommendation:** Do not scale the pilot now. Authorize Sam to run a matched-ticket follow-up.

## What the results support

The pilot handled 100 tickets at a mean of 12 minutes, compared with 15 minutes across 200 baseline tickets. That is a 3-minute (20%) difference. The two groups had different ticket mixes, and tickets were not randomly allocated, so the difference could come from easier tickets reaching the pilot. The results do not show that the pilot caused faster handling.

Quality fell from a 94% pass rate at baseline to 89% in the pilot. The scaling threshold is at least 92%, so the pilot fails this requirement by 3 points regardless of the handling-time result.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured. Any financial case for scaling would rest on assumptions the pilot did not test.

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot | Scaling requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tickets | 200 | 100 | None stated |
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min | None stated |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% | At least 92% |
| Measured savings | None | None | None stated |

## Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up. Matching tickets would remove the ticket-mix difference, so the follow-up could show whether the 3-minute gap holds on comparable work. It would also produce a new quality pass rate to test against the 92% threshold.

If the COO authorizes the follow-up, a scale decision should wait for its results on handling time and quality. If the COO declines, the current evidence does not meet the quality threshold and gives no measured savings, so the pilot should not be scaled.
