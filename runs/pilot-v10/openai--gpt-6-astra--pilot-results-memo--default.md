## Executive readout: Do not scale now

The pilot showed lower mean handling time, but it did not meet the required quality threshold. The results support a matched-ticket follow-up—not scaling or a claim of proven efficiency gains.

### What the results support
- **Handling time improved descriptively:** Mean handling time was 12 minutes across 100 pilot tickets versus 15 minutes across 200 baseline tickets—a 3-minute, or 20%, reduction. Because ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized, this comparison does not establish that the pilot caused the reduction.
- **Quality is below the scaling requirement:** The pilot quality pass rate was 89%, versus 94% at baseline and a required scaling threshold of at least 92%. The pilot therefore misses the threshold by 3 percentage points.
- **Financial benefits remain unproven:** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured. Lower handling time should not be presented as realized savings.

### Recommendation and next decision
**Do not scale the pilot now.** Ask the COO to authorize a matched-ticket follow-up, which Sam can run.

Use that follow-up to assess whether the handling-time advantage persists with comparable tickets and whether quality meets the 92% threshold. Revisit scaling after reviewing those results; evaluate any financial benefit separately rather than assuming time reductions translate into cash savings.
