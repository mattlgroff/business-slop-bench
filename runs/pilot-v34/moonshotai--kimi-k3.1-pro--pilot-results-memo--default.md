# Executive Readout: Do Not Scale the Pilot Now

## Recommendation
**Do not authorize scaling.** The pilot provides a preliminary handling-time signal, but it does not meet the required quality threshold or establish cash savings.

## What the Results Support
- **Observed speed improvement:** Mean handling time was 12 minutes across 100 pilot tickets, versus 15 minutes across 200 baseline tickets—an observed reduction of 3 minutes, or 20%.
- **No causal conclusion yet:** Ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized. The results therefore do not establish that the pilot approach caused the improvement or that it will generalize at scale.
- **Quality is below the scaling gate:** The quality pass rate fell from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot. This is 5 percentage points lower and 3 points below the required minimum of 92%.
- **No demonstrated financial case:** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured.

Overall, the evidence supports further evaluation—not a scale-up. Scaling now would proceed despite confounded handling-time results, sub-threshold quality, and unquantified economic benefit.

## Next Decision
The **COO should decide whether to authorize Sam’s matched-ticket follow-up. We recommend authorizing it.** The follow-up should provide a better basis for reassessing handling-time performance using matched tickets. Scaling should remain on hold pending that reassessment, with quality measured against the at-least-92% threshold and the absence of measured staffing or cash savings addressed before the next scale decision.
