# Executive readout: pilot scaling decision

**Recommendation: Do not scale the pilot now. The COO should authorize Sam to run the matched-ticket follow-up.**

## What the results support

- Mean handling time was 12 minutes across 100 pilot tickets, compared with 15 minutes across 200 baseline tickets: an observed reduction of 3 minutes, or 20%.
- Ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized. The results therefore do not establish that the pilot caused the reduction or that the same reduction would hold at scale.
- Quality moved in the wrong direction. The pass rate fell from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot, a 5 percentage point decline. The pilot missed the required scaling threshold of at least 92% by 3 percentage points.
- No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so the results do not support a financial case for scaling.

The evidence supports further evaluation through the proposed follow-up. It does not support scaling now, given the quality threshold miss and the limits on interpreting the handling-time result.

## Next decision

The COO should decide whether to authorize the follow-up; the recommended decision is yes. Sam should run the matched-ticket comparison to reassess handling time across comparable tickets and check whether quality reaches the scaling threshold. Scaling should remain paused pending those results.
