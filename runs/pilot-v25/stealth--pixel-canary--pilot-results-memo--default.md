

# Pilot Readout: Do Not Scale

## Recommendation

**Do not scale.** Quality fails the required threshold and the efficiency result is not attributable to the pilot intervention.

## What the Results Support

| Metric | Baseline | Pilot | Verdict |
|--------|----------|-------|---------|
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min | Suggestive, not causal |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% | Fails 92% threshold |
| Measured cost savings | — | None | Not demonstrated |

The 3-minute reduction in handling time is directionally encouraging, but the pilot lacked randomization and the ticket mix differed from baseline. The improvement could reflect easier tickets rather than a real process gain.

Quality is the decisive issue. At 89%, the pilot is 3 points below the 92% scaling floor. Scaling now would expose the operation to a quality gap that is currently unexplained and unmitigated.

## Why Not Scale Yet

1. **Quality below threshold.** 89% < 92%. This is a hard gate.
2. **No causal claim on speed.** Without randomization or matched comparisons, the handling-time drop is a hypothesis, not a finding.
3. **No financial proof.** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so the business case rests entirely on unvalidated efficiency gains.

## Next Decision

Sam can run a matched-ticket follow-up that controls for mix and produces a defensible efficiency comparison while isolating the quality gap. This study requires COO authorization.

**Proposed action:** Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up. Revisit the scale decision only if quality clears 92% and the follow-up confirms a genuine handling-time improvement.
