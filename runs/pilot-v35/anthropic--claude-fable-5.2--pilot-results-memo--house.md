# Pilot readout: do not scale now

## Recommendation

Hold scaling. The pilot's quality pass rate was 89%, below the 92% threshold required to scale, and the handling-time result cannot yet be attributed to the pilot.

## What the results support

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Tickets | 200 | 100 |
| Mean handling time | 15 minutes | 12 minutes |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% |

Pilot tickets took 3 minutes less on average, a 20% reduction. Two design limits prevent reading that as a proven effect: the ticket mix differed from baseline, and tickets were not randomly allocated. Easier tickets in the pilot group could explain some or all of the gap.

Quality fell 5 points. The same design limits apply here, so the pilot does not show whether the new process caused the drop. The 89% figure still fails the scaling requirement as measured.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured. The time reduction has no financial value attached to it yet.

## What the results do not support

- A claim that the pilot process cuts handling time by 20%.
- A claim that the pilot meets the quality requirement.
- Any savings figure.

## Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize a matched-ticket follow-up, which Sam can run. Comparing like tickets would remove the ticket-mix difference and show whether the time reduction and the quality drop hold.

I recommend authorizing it. Revisit scaling if the follow-up shows a quality pass rate of at least 92% and a handling-time reduction on matched tickets. If quality stays below 92%, the pilot process needs changes before scaling is reconsidered.
