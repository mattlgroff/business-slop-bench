# Executive Readout: Pilot Scale Decision

**Recommendation: Do not scale the pilot now.**

## What the Results Support

- **Handling time fell from 15 to 12 minutes**, based on 100 pilot tickets versus 200 baseline tickets. This is encouraging but not attributable to the pilot, because the ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized.
- **Quality fell from 94% to 89%**, below the 92% scaling threshold. This fails the gate on its own.
- **No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured**, so the financial case is unproven.

## What the Results Do Not Support

- A causal claim that the pilot reduced handling time
- Any claim of cost savings or released capacity
- Scaling, given the quality shortfall

| Criterion | Result | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Handling time | 15 → 12 min | Not causally established |
| Quality pass rate | 94% → 89% | Fails (≥92% required) |
| Cost impact | Not measured | Unproven |

## Next Decision

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up. **Recommended: authorize it.** The follow-up would test the handling-time gain on comparable tickets and examine the quality drop. Revisit scaling only if the follow-up shows quality at or above 92% and a handling-time gain that holds under matched conditions.
