**Recommendation: Do not scale now. Authorize a matched-ticket follow-up.**

The pilot cut mean handling time from 15 to 12 minutes per ticket. The results do not support scaling yet, for two reasons.

First, the comparison is unreliable. The pilot's 100 tickets had a different mix from the 200-ticket baseline, and allocation was not randomized. Part or all of the 3-minute gain could come from easier tickets rather than from the change itself.

Second, quality fell below the scaling threshold. Pass rate dropped from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot, against a required minimum of 92%. Speed gains that come with lower pass rates would shift rework onto other teams.

Costs are also unmeasured. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been recorded, so there is no financial case to scale against.

**Next decision**

The COO decides whether to authorize a matched-ticket follow-up run by Sam. The follow-up would compare tickets with a matching mix, giving a clean read on handling time and quality together. If the follow-up holds a 3-minute gain with a pass rate at or above 92%, the COO can then approve a scaled rollout.
