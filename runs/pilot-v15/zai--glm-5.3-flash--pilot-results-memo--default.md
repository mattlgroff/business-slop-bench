**Executive Readout: Handle-Time Pilot — Scale Decision**

**Recommendation:** Do not scale now. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up before any scaling decision.

**What the results support:**
- The pilot showed a faster mean handling time: 12 minutes on 100 pilot tickets versus 15 minutes at baseline (200 tickets).
- The efficiency signal is credible enough to justify one more, better-controlled test.

**What the results do not support:**
- **No causal claim.** Ticket mix differed between pilot and baseline, and allocation was not randomized. The 3-minute improvement may reflect easier tickets rather than the intervention.
- **Quality regression.** Pass rate fell from 94% (baseline) to 89% (pilot), below the required 92% scaling threshold. On current data, the pilot fails the quality gate.
- **No financial case.** No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no basis for a return-on-investment claim.

**Next decision:**
- **COO decision required:** whether to authorize Sam's proposed matched-ticket follow-up.
- The follow-up should match ticket mix across groups (or randomize allocation) so the handling-time effect can be attributed to the intervention, and quality should be re-measured against the 92% threshold.

**Decision summary:** Results support continuing evaluation via the matched-ticket follow-up. They do not support scaling now, given non-randomized allocation, a quality pass rate below threshold, and unmeasured savings. Scaling should be revisited only if the follow-up confirms the time savings and restores quality above 92%.
