**Executive Readout: Do Not Scale the Pilot Yet**

**Recommendation:** Do not scale now. The pilot shows a promising speed signal but fails the required quality threshold and cannot support a scaling decision.

**What the results support**

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Tickets | 200 | 100 |
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% |

The pilot’s mean handling time was 3 minutes lower (12 vs. 15), a directional efficiency signal. However, this result is not decision-grade. Ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized, so the difference may reflect which tickets were handled rather than a real effect.

Quality is a harder stop. The pilot’s 89% pass rate is below the required scaling threshold of at least 92% and below the 94% baseline. Scaling would therefore violate the stated threshold.

Costs do not compensate. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no verified financial case for scaling.

**What the results do not support**

They do not support scaling, a full rollout, or claims of savings. They also do not prove the pilot caused slower quality or faster handling.

**Next decision**

The COO should decide whether to authorize Sam’s matched-ticket follow-up. That follow-up should test handling time and quality on comparable tickets with a valid comparison. Authorize it only if the COO wants stronger evidence before any scaling decision.

**Decision rule for the follow-up**

Proceed toward scaling only if quality meets or exceeds 92% and handling-time gains hold on matched tickets. Otherwise, stop or redesign.
