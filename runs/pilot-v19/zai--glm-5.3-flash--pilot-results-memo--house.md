# Executive readout: automation pilot scale decision

**Recommendation: Do not scale now. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up.**

## What the results support

The pilot cut mean handling time from 15 minutes (baseline, 200 tickets) to 12 minutes (pilot, 100 tickets), a 3-minute reduction. Two factors limit what we can conclude from it:

1. **Allocation was not randomized** and the ticket mix differed between groups, so the time savings cannot be attributed to the tool with confidence. Some or all of the 3 minutes may reflect easier tickets in the pilot group.
2. **Quality fell below the scaling threshold.** Pass rate dropped from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot, against a required minimum of 92%.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so there is no cost case to weigh yet.

## Next decision

The COO should decide whether to authorize Sam's proposed matched-ticket follow-up. The follow-up addresses the design flaw: comparing tickets matched on type gives a cleaner read on whether the 3-minute saving is real.

## What the follow-up must show before scaling

- Handling time savings hold on matched tickets.
- Quality pass rate recovers to at least 92%.
- A measured staffing or cash impact that supports the cost case.

If the follow-up meets these, scaling returns to this table with an evidence-backed proposal. If quality stays at 89% or the savings disappear, the pilot should be stopped.
