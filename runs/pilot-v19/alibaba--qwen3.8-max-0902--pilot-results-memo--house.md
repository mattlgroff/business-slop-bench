**Executive Readout: Pilot Scaling Decision**

**Recommendation: Do not scale. Authorize the matched-ticket follow-up.**

**What the pilot showed**

Mean handling time dropped from 15 minutes (baseline, 200 tickets) to 12 minutes (pilot, 100 tickets). The ticket mix differed between the two groups and allocation was not randomized, so the 3-minute improvement cannot be attributed to the intervention with confidence.

Quality pass rate fell from 94% to 89%. The required scaling threshold is 92%. The pilot does not meet it.

No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured.

**Why scaling now is unsupported**

Two independent problems block a scaling decision:

1. The quality result is below the stated threshold by three points.
2. The speed result is unreliable because the comparison groups were not equivalent.

Either finding alone would be sufficient to hold. Together they make the case clear.

**Next decision**

Sam can run a matched-ticket follow-up that controls for ticket mix and uses randomized or matched allocation. The COO decides whether to authorize that work.

If the follow-up confirms both a quality pass rate at or above 92% and a real handling-time reduction, scaling can return for approval with cost data.
