**Executive Readout: Do Not Scale the Pilot Now**

**Recommendation:** Do not authorize scaling. The pilot results do not support expansion, and the quality data points to a regression.

**What the results support**

Handling time fell from a 15 minute mean at baseline to 12 minutes in the pilot. That improvement is real but cannot be attributed to the pilot. Ticket mix differed between the two groups, and allocation was not randomized. The observed difference may reflect which tickets were handled, not how they were handled.

Quality moved in the wrong direction. The pass rate fell from 94% at baseline to 89% in the pilot. The required scaling threshold is at least 92%. The pilot missed that threshold by three points.

Costs also remain unproven. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been measured, so the pilot offers no evidence of financial return.

**Assessment**

The pilot fails the stated quality gate. The time result is confounded and cannot carry a scaling decision on its own. Scaling now would commit resources against a quality regression and an unverified cost case.

**Next decision**

The COO decides whether to authorize Sam's matched-ticket follow-up. That study would compare like tickets under randomized allocation and measure the quality pass rate against the 92% threshold.

Authorize the follow-up only if the COO wants a defensible answer on both time and quality before any scaling commitment. If the COO declines, the pilot should not be scaled on the current evidence.
