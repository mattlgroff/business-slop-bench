Recommendation: Do not scale the pilot now. Authorize a matched-ticket follow-up first.

What the results show

| Measure | Baseline | Pilot |
|---|---|---|
| Tickets | 200 | 100 |
| Mean handling time | 15 min | 12 min |
| Quality pass rate | 94% | 89% |

Why scaling is not supported

- Quality fails the gate. The pilot pass rate of 89% is below the required scaling threshold of 92%, and 5 points below baseline.
- The speed gain is unproven. Handling time was 3 minutes (20%) lower in the pilot, but the ticket mix differed and allocation was not randomized. The difference may come from easier tickets rather than the pilot.
- No financial benefit is measured. No staffing reductions or cash savings have been recorded, so the pilot has no demonstrated return.

What the results support

The pilot is a candidate for further testing. The shorter handling time is a signal worth checking, and the quality shortfall is a defect to investigate. Neither result is reliable enough to justify a rollout.

Next decision

The COO decides whether to authorize a matched-ticket follow-up. Sam can run it. Matching pilot and baseline tickets by type removes the mix difference, so the follow-up can test whether the time saving holds and whether quality reaches 92%.

Proposed scaling criteria for the follow-up result:
- Quality pass rate of at least 92% on matched tickets.
- Handling time still lower than baseline on matched tickets.
- A measured staffing or cost effect, if the COO wants savings to justify scaling.

If quality stays below 92%, the pilot should not scale in its current form.
