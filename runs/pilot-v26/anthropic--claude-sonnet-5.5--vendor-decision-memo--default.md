**Recommendation**
Select Alpha. It is the only vendor that meets the mandatory SSO requirement and it fits within the year-one budget ceiling.

**Cost comparison (year one)**

| | Alpha | Beta |
|---|---|---|
| Annual subscription | $72,000 | $60,000 |
| One-time setup | $20,000 | $10,000 |
| **Year-one total** | **$92,000** | **$70,000** |
| Budget ceiling | $100,000 | $100,000 |
| Headroom | $8,000 | $30,000 |
| SSO available | Yes | No, no committed date |
| Other requirements | Met | Met |

**Evaluation against constraints**

1. **Budget ($100,000 including setup):** Both vendors are within the ceiling. Alpha leaves $8,000 of headroom, and Beta leaves $30,000.
2. **SSO mandatory before production:** Alpha satisfies this. Beta does not, and it has offered no committed delivery date. Beta therefore cannot go to production under the stated rule, and we cannot plan around an undated commitment.
3. **Other requirements:** Both vendors meet them, so they do not differentiate the options.

**Rationale**
Beta is $22,000 cheaper in year one, but the savings do not matter if the product cannot be used in production. SSO is a gating requirement, not a preference. Choosing Beta would mean paying for a product that cannot launch, with no date for when it could. Alpha's $92,000 total is within budget.

**Risks and considerations**
- Alpha's headroom is modest ($8,000), so scope changes or added costs beyond the quoted figures could pressure the ceiling. The source pack does not describe any other costs.
- Beta's price advantage would matter only if it delivered SSO on a committed date. The pack contains no such commitment.

**Next steps**
1. CFO approves Alpha at $92,000 for year one.
2. Priya proceeds to contract signing after approval.
3. Priya should confirm in the contract that SSO is included.
